# TM5 adjoint controls to mirror first

This note records what TM5-4DVAR uses its adjoint for, so the first
AtmosTransport adjoint work starts with the right control variables.

## What TM5 computes adjoints for

- **Surface emissions are the primary control.** The 2008 TM5-4DVAR
  methane paper presents the system as a way to optimize methane
  emissions with a large control vector, and later applications optimize
  emissions at individual grid-cell and time-step resolution.
- **Initial concentrations are part of the control-vector design.**
  Meirink et al. split the background covariance into mutually
  uncorrelated blocks for emission source categories, the initial
  concentration field, and additional parameters.
- **Additional chemical or bias parameters can be active controls.**
  TM5 adjoint work has optimized or diagnosed sensitivity to OH
  concentrations, and newer TM5-4DVAR applications add problem-specific
  controls such as satellite bias terms or nonlinear emission-deviation
  factors.
- **The adjoint covers the transport/physics operators, not meteorology
  as a routine inversion control.** Published TM5 inversions treat
  meteorological fields as offline drivers. The adjoint transposes
  transport, vertical diffusion, convection, chemistry/loss, and source
  insertion so gradients can be accumulated onto the chosen state-vector
  elements.

## Implementation details worth copying

- The TM5 adjoint was hand-coded by matrix transposition except for the
  slopes advection scheme, whose adjoint was initially generated by TAMC.
  Meirink et al. call out vertical diffusion, convection, OH oxidation,
  and emissions as straightforward adjoint pieces.
- TM5 verified the discrete adjoint with the identity
  `<M x, y> = <x, M^T y>` to machine precision for windows up to about a
  year.
- In the methane tracer version, TM5 disabled advection limiters because
  the limiters are nonlinear and were unnecessary for CH4. That made the
  forward operator linear enough for exact matrix-transpose tests.
- Krol et al.'s 14CO/OH setup stores the forward tracer field during the
  forward run because the adjoint OH accumulation needs products of
  forward and adjoint tracer fields.

## AtmosTransport first-step implication

The first production target should be gradients with respect to
**surface-emission rates** on the CS grid, followed by initial tracer
mass. That matches the inverse-system need and the TM5 control-vector
pattern. For PPM tests, use `PPMScheme(NoLimiter())` first so the
linearity and transpose identity are meaningful; limited PPM should be
treated as a piecewise-linear tangent/adjoint around a stored forward
trajectory.
